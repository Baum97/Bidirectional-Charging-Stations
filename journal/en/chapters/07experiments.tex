\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}

The experiment is conducted on mobility data, which is generated by an algorithm that simulates movement patterns of agents in a given area. The algorithm takes into account various factors such as land use, transportation infrastructure and natural behavior of agents to generate realistic mobility data. The generated data is then used to evaluate the performance of the proposed charging station placement algorithm. Before running the simulation, the dataset consists of surface level mobility (agent start- and endpoints, OSM-data), which is used for traffic flow, POIs and electrical grid information. When a simulation is run, the dataset is enriched with additional information such as charging behavior, energy consumption, specific locations of agents and their SoC. The datasets are generated using a combination of real-world data and synthetic data, ensuring that the results are both realistic and generalizable to other urban areas. The datasets are also designed to be scalable, allowing for the evaluation of the placement algorithms on larger areas with more agents and charging stations.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

In a one kilometer by one kilometer area in Berlin, Germany, the experiment is conducted with a total of 1000 agents, which are randomly distributed across the area's residential zones. The agents are assigned a set of activities and destinations based on the generated trip chains, which are used to simulate their movement patterns throughout the day. The simulation is run for a total of 1 hour, with a time step of 10 seconds, to reduce the computational load and allow for a more efficient evaluation of the placement algorithm. The simulation is run multiple times with different random seeds to ensure that the results are statistically significant and not biased by any particular configuration. To create a comparison between the two placement strategies, the experiment is run with both the Saturate-and-Prune and Clustering algorithms, and the results are compared based on the evaluation metrics defined in \cref{subsec:eval_metrics}. Throughout the experiment, both approaches are evaluated on trials with identical parameters, due to dynamic routing small differences in the agents' behavior may occur, which can lead to noise in the results. To mitigate this, the experiment is repeated multiple times with different random seeds, and the results are averaged to provide a more robust evaluation of the placement algorithms. The algorithms are designed to be scalable and reproducible, allowing for their application to larger areas and different urban contexts. The results of the experiment are analyzed and visualized to provide insights into the performance of the placement algorithms and their impact on mobility accessibility and grid stress.
